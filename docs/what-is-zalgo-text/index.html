What is zalgo text?

Zalgo text is ordinary text with many combining marks added to each letter. The marks pile up above and below the line until the words look as if they are falling apart. It also goes by glitch text and cursed text.

Combining marks

A combining mark is a character that has no place of its own on the line. It is drawn on the letter before it. That is how accents can be written: the letter e followed by the mark U+0301 shows as é.

The marks zalgo text uses are the accents, dots, bars and rings from U+0300 to U+036F. Some sit above the letter, some below, and a few run through it.

How the marks stack

A letter can be followed by more than one mark. A font that knows how to stack them puts each new mark above the one before, or below it for the marks that hang under the line. Twenty marks above a letter make a pile twenty marks tall.

A pile that tall does not fit in its line. It spills over the lines above and below, which is the whole look of zalgo text and also its main problem: many sites cut off whatever leaves the post.

Why it looks different on every device

The text is the same everywhere. What changes is the font that draws it, and fonts do not agree.

  • How far each mark moves the pile differs from font to font.
  • Some fonts have every mark but do not stack them. This is the tool's warning for Slack: Slack's own font (Lato) has every mark but draws them all at one height, so a pile on any ordinary letter comes out as a flat line. A pile only stacks on ‧ or ∆, which Lato lacks.
  • The same text can differ from one browser to the next on the same site. The tool's warning for X: A pile on a blank or an ordinary letter comes out flat on X in Firefox: X's own font draws the letter and the marks are left with nothing to stack on. Chrome and Safari stack it. Only a column standing on ˍ stacks in all three.
  • Safari and the iPhone, iPad and Mac apps stack only the first 30 marks on a letter.
  • Some apps stop much sooner. The tool's note on Telegram: Telegram Desktop draws at most 2 marks per letter, so pictures only have a chance in the phone apps.

So a pile that stands tall on one screen may sit lower, come out flat or not stack at all on another.

How it is counted

Every mark is a character of its own, so zalgo text is much longer than it looks. A letter with twenty marks is one letter to the reader and twenty-one characters to most platforms.

Platforms do not count the same way either. Some count each mark, some count bytes, and some count a letter with all its marks as one. The guide to character limits by platform has the figure for each.

From noise to pictures

Most zalgo text is random: the more marks, the more broken it looks. But a pile has a height, and marks come in different weights. Double bars are dark, dots are mid, ticks are light.

That is enough to draw with. Give every column of a picture one letter, pile marks on it as high as the picture is at that column, and choose light or heavy marks for the shade. The result is zalgo art: a picture, a drawing or big lettering that is still one line of text.

Zalgo Art makes it from an image, from words or from a drawing, and checks that it will fit and arrive whole where you post it. For how that compares with pictures made of ordinary characters, read zalgo art and ASCII art.
